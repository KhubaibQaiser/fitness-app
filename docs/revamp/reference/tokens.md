# GymOS design tokens: colour, type and glass

Status: **proposed**. Khubaib decides; nothing here is accepted until he rules.
Date: 2026-11-23. Built for the UI revamp from the audit report's direction D, "Gym Floor Print".

Every hex value, contrast ratio and colour-vision number in this file is generated by `scripts/tokens-build.mjs` from the palette recipe in `scripts/tokens-palettes.mjs`. To regenerate after a change: `node scripts/tokens-build.mjs --emit` (it exits non-zero if any required text or control pair drops below WCAG AA). The same run writes `tokens.json` and `report/tokens.html`.

Terms used throughout, defined once:

- **OKLCH**: a way of writing a colour as Lightness (0 = black, 1 = white), Chroma (how colourful; 0 = grey) and hue angle (0 to 360). Unlike hex or HSL, equal steps of L look like equal steps of lightness to people, which is why scales built in it behave predictably. CSS supports it natively.
- **WCAG 2.x contrast ratio**: the accessibility standard's measure, from 1:1 to 21:1. Body text needs 4.5:1, large text and control boundaries need 3:1.
- **APCA Lc**: a newer contrast measure (Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm) that models how people actually perceive text on a background. It is signed: positive is dark text on light, negative is light text on dark. Rough guide: |Lc| 75 for body text, 60 for other content text, 45 for large headings, 30 for non-text. It is not a WCAG requirement yet; I report it as a second opinion.
- **CVD**: colour-vision deficiency. Red-green deficiencies (protan and deutan types) are commonly cited at about 1 in 12 men of Northern European descent; I did not look up a Pakistan-specific figure, so treat that as indicative only. Tritanopia (blue-yellow) is rare.
- **Delta E (Oklab x100)**: the distance between two colours in the Oklab space, multiplied by 100. Below about 6 two colours are hard to tell apart; 8 or more is comfortable.

## 1. Summary and recommendation

**Recommended palette: Option 1, "Gym Floor Print, evolved".** It keeps the soul of the login illustration (cobalt, ochre, olive, sand), raises chroma on the inks (cobalt +39 percent, olive to moss +66 percent) and lifts the ochre from a dark mustard (L 0.62) to a bright marigold (L 0.77, +19 percent chroma, which is the sRGB ceiling for that hue at that lightness), while the ground stays almost neutral. Cobalt is the action colour; marigold (the evolved ochre), moss (the evolved olive) and plum are data and highlight inks; four status hues sit in the gaps between those inks; warm sand paper becomes blue-black ink in dark mode.

**Alternative: Option 2, "Floodlight".** Electric ultramarine, volt lime and aqua on cool graphite, dark-first. Bolder and more "2026 sports app", weaker in light mode and for tenant colours.

**Colour theory in two sentences.** A complementary axis (cobalt at hue 262 against marigold at hue 75, 184 degrees apart) sits on an analogous warm ground (sand, marigold and moss all fall between hue 75 and 126), so the one cool ink pops against the paper without needing more saturation. Colour volume follows urgency: neutrals cover most of the screen, vivid ink is spent only on the action, on states that need the coach, and on data.

**Glass verdict.** Worth offering as a chrome-only option (top bars, tab bar, client header, sheets on iOS), never on tables, forms or data cards. Legible glass is mostly frosted: over a worst-case backdrop the light chrome needs 66 percent tint and a light sheet needs 92 percent. It is close to free on iOS 26 through native tabs and system sheets, worth it on web with a solid fallback, and should stay off on Android until it is tested on a low-end device.

**Type.** Inter for UI (already wired on web and native), Archivo at expanded widths for page titles and hero numbers (sporty, scoreboard-like, has tabular figures), Noto Nastaliq Urdu for Urdu reading text and headings, Noto Naskh Arabic for dense Urdu UI. All four are on Google Fonts under the SIL Open Font License. Roboto Mono retires: numbers use Inter's tabular figures.

Open decisions are batched in section 12.

## 2. Token architecture

### 2.1 Three layers

| Layer     | What it holds                                                                              | Example                                                 | Who changes it                                                      |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primitive | Raw values with no meaning: 13 neutral steps, 11 steps per hue, spacing, radius, durations | `cobalt.700 = #2660DF`, `space.4 = 16px`                | Design system owner, rarely                                         |
| Semantic  | Meaning, per theme. Components only ever read these.                                       | `action.bg`, `fg.secondary`, `status.danger.wash`       | Theme switch (light, dark, glass), tenant brand for `action.*` only |
| Component | Per-component decisions that reference semantic tokens                                     | `button.primary.bg = {action.bg}`, `chip.severeAllergy` | Component author                                                    |

Naming: `category.role.variant`, lower camel case inside a segment. Semantic categories: `bg` (surfaces), `fg` (text and icons), `border`, `action` (the tenant brand slot), `focus`, `status.{success,warning,danger,info}`, `accent.{family}`, `viz`, `scrim`, `glass`. No token name contains a colour word at the semantic layer, so a theme can remap it.

### 2.2 Mapping to Tamagui

Tamagui themes are flat key maps, so the semantic path flattens to camel case: `bg.canvas` becomes `$bgCanvas`, `status.danger.washText` becomes `$statusDangerWashText`. Proposed theme tree:

- `light`, `dark`: every semantic colour token. Primitives go in `tokens.color` so components can still reach a raw step for illustration work.
- `light_glass`, `dark_glass`: sub-themes that override only `chromeBg`, `chromeBorder`, `sheetBg` (in the solid themes those keys equal `bgSurface` / `bgRaised`). Chrome components (`AppTopBar`, `TabBar`, `ClientHeader`, `Sheet`) always read `$chromeBg`; wrapping them in `<Theme name="glass">` turns glass on. The blur itself is a component concern (CSS `backdrop-filter` on web, `BlurView` or `GlassView` on native), driven by the `glass.blur.*` tokens.
- Tenant brand: the `action.*` keys of `light` and `dark` are generated at runtime from the tenant's one colour (2.4). Today `tamagui.config.ts` imports `pilot.json` at build time and only `primary` flows into `accentBg`; runtime themes need either Tamagui's runtime theme API (`addTheme`/`updateTheme` per the research doc, **unverified** in Tamagui 2 this session) or, on web, CSS variables that the compiled theme reads.
- Migration: keep a `legacyAliases` block that points the old names at the new ones for one release (`canvas → bgCanvas`, `surface → bgSurface`, `textPrimary → fgPrimary`, `textSecondary → fgSecondary`, `textFaint → fgFaint`, `accentBg → actionBg`, `accentText → actionText`, `accentWash → actionWash`, `alertText → statusDangerText`, `alertWash → statusDangerWash`, `milestoneFill → accentMarigoldFill`, `success → statusSuccessSolid`, `warning → statusWarningSolid`, `danger → statusDangerSolid`), then delete it. The current `light_client` / `dark_client` rose themes have no consumer in a coach-only product; I propose dropping them rather than porting them.

### 2.3 What the themes contain

Light and dark each define: `bg.canvas, surface, raised, sunken, hover, field, inverse`; `fg.primary, secondary, faint, disabled, inverse`; `border.subtle, control`; `action.bg, bgHover, bgPressed, fg, text, wash, washText`; `focus.ring`; `scrim`; `status.{4}.{solid, onSolid, text, wash, washText, border}`; `accent.{3}.{fill, onFill, strong, text, washText, wash}`; `viz.{grid, axis, axisLabel, trendLine, goalBand, goalLine, categorical.1-6, macro.*, sequential.1-7, diverging.1-7}`. Glass adds `glass.{chrome, sheet, border, edge, fallback}` plus blur, saturation, highlight, shadow and noise values. Full mapping in section 5; `tokens.json` has all of it in machine form.

Dark mode is composed, not inverted: three distinct surface steps (canvas `neutral.950`, surface `neutral.900`, raised `neutral.850`, a 0.055 lightness step each, where the current app has cards at 1.06:1 on canvas), a lighter action text (`cobalt.400`), and status washes that are deep tints rather than pale ones.

### 2.4 Tenant brand at runtime

A tenant supplies one hex (`brand.primary`, already in `infra/tenants/pilot.json`) and a radius preset. The app derives everything else, in OKLCH, keeping the tenant's hue:

1. **Validate** the hex at the manifest boundary; an invalid value throws (no silent default).
2. **Action fill.** If white or ink text on the exact brand colour reaches 4.5:1, use the brand unchanged, so the tenant sees their own colour. Otherwise lower L in steps of 0.005 at the same hue and chroma until white text reaches 4.5:1, and log the adjustment.
3. **On-colour**: white or ink (`fg.primary`), whichever contrasts more. Always 4.5:1 or better by construction.
4. **Hover and pressed**: L minus 0.04 and 0.08.
5. **Text-safe tone per mode** (links, selected labels, the focus ring): start at the brand and move L away from the surface (darker in light mode, lighter in dark) until it reaches 4.5:1 on both surface and canvas.
6. **Wash per mode** (selected row, selected tab pill): L 0.95 light / 0.29 dark at a third of the brand's chroma, with a wash-text tone stepped until 4.5:1.
7. **Boundary check**: if the fill is under 3:1 against the surface (a pale or yellow brand), buttons get a 1px border in the text-safe tone (WCAG 1.4.11).
8. **Collision check** against the reserved status colours, by Oklab Delta E under normal vision and under protan/deutan simulation.

**Guardrails.** A tenant can never set `bg.*`, `fg.*`, `border.*`, `status.*`, `accent.*`, `viz.*`, `glass.*` or `scrim`. Chart colours are fixed GymOS primitives, not the brand, so a red tenant never repaints a weight chart. The severe-allergy chip is always `status.danger.solid` with an octagon icon and the words, and it is never tinted by the brand or by glass.

**Red-brand policy (decision).** If the brand is within Delta E 15 of `status.danger.solid` under normal vision, a red primary button would sit beside a red allergy warning. Recommended: filled actions switch to the inverse neutral (`bg.inverse` fill, `fg.inverse` label), links and the focus ring use `fg.primary`, selection washes use `bg.hover`, and the brand stays on the logo and app icon. A red link or a red-tinted selected row next to a red flag chip is the same confusion in a smaller dose. Alternatives: keep brand actions and rely on icon and shape alone, or ask the tenant to pick another action colour at onboarding.

## 3. Colour theory

### 3.1 The harmony model

The login illustration already contains the scheme; it just needed naming and a ladder.

- **One cool ink against a warm analogous family.** Cobalt (hue 262) is the near-complement of marigold (hue 75): 184 degrees apart. Marigold, moss (hue 126) and the sand neutrals (hue 72 to 85) form an analogous warm family. Complementary pairs give the most energy two hues can have; keeping one side to a single ink stops it turning into a circus. [opinion, standard colour theory]
- **Simultaneous contrast does the vibrancy work.** On warm sand, cobalt reads cooler and more saturated than its numbers; on blue-black, marigold and moss glow. So the inks can stay at earth-anchored chroma (0.15 to 0.21) and still look vivid. This is why the neutrals shift temperature: warm paper in the light half of the scale (hue 72 to 85), cool blue-black ink in the dark half (hue 262). Light mode is ink on paper; dark mode is a lit court at night.
- **Status hues sit in the gaps between inks.** Reading round the wheel: vermilion (danger) 26, tangerine (warning) 53, _marigold_ 75, _moss_ 126, pitch green (success) 154, lagoon (info) 224, _cobalt_ 262, _plum_ 348. Each status hue is at least 22 degrees from the nearest ink, and is used with a different treatment (chips with icons) from the inks (fills, data marks), so a marigold chart bar never reads as a warning.
- **Plum is the third accent**, added because a print palette with only warm inks and one blue cannot carry six chart series. It is the illustration's missing "red ink", kept away from vermilion (hue 348 against 26) so it never reads as danger.

### 3.3 Why it reads vibrant but calm for an all-day tool

1. **Chroma is concentrated, not spread.** Neutrals carry chroma at or below 0.014; inks carry 0.15 to 0.21. A screen of a dozen queue rows shows two or three saturated marks, not twelve.
2. **Colour volume follows urgency.** States that need the coach (overdue, red flag, needs review) get a filled wash chip; calm states (on track, published) get an icon and a word in the status text colour with no fill; draft gets a dashed neutral outline; severe allergy is the only solid red chip. The eye goes to what needs work.
3. **One lightness ladder for every hue.** Step 700 has L 0.53 in cobalt, marigold and vermilion alike, so no hue shouts by being lighter or darker than its neighbours, and the contrast of a step is predictable across families.
4. **Earth-anchored inks.** Ochre, olive and plum are print inks, not neon. The one bright family is cobalt, and it is spent on the action.

### 3.5 Emotional read in this market [opinion, not user-tested]

- Green reads strongly positive in Pakistan (national flag, religious association), so success green lands naturally; pitch green is deliberately a fresh, slightly blue green rather than a flag green, so it reads as "healthy" rather than patriotic.
- Blue reads as institutional trust (banking and telecom apps commonly use it), which suits a professional coach tool and is the tenant default anyway.
- Marigold and ochre carry warmth (wheat, haldi, gold) without the "cream and terracotta" look that the audit flagged as an AI cliché.
- Red is safety-critical here because of allergies, so it is rationed: one solid red element, and no red brand actions (2.4).

I have not tested these reads with coaches. Five short sessions with Pakistani coaches showing both options would settle it more than this paragraph can.

### 3.6 Colour-vision check

Method: every pair was simulated with the Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes (2009) model at severity 1.0 for protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia, and measured as Oklab Delta E x100 (code in `scripts/tokens-colorlib.mjs`). The categorical palettes were then cross-checked with an independent validator (the dataviz skill's `validate_palette.js`, same simulation model, which also checks lightness band, chroma floor, a normal-vision floor of 15 and 3:1 contrast). Its raw output is at the end of section 7.

What it found and what I changed:

- At equal lightness, protan and deutan vision collapse every hue onto one blue-yellow axis: plum and teal measured Delta E 1.1, marigold and moss 0.9. So the chart slots alternate between three lightness levels per mode (0.45 / 0.55 / 0.64 light, 0.53 / 0.60 / 0.665 dark), and the order was found by enumerating all 120 orders with cobalt first and every lightness combination. Result: worst adjacent CVD Delta E 14.9 light and 14.2 dark (target 8); the first four slots (the macros) clear 14.9 light and 8.8 dark across all pairs.
- Status colours cannot all be told apart under CVD, by nature (red and green collapse). The table shows exactly where. That is acceptable only because status always carries an icon, a word and a shape (3.4); hue is the fourth channel, not the first.

## 4. Palettes

### 4.1 How the scales are built

Every chromatic family uses the same 11-step lightness curve: 50 `0.975`, 100 `0.948`, 200 `0.905`, 300 `0.845`, 400 `0.77`, 500 `0.69`, 600 `0.60`, 700 `0.53`, 800 `0.45`, 900 `0.37`, 950 `0.285`. Chroma rises from 12 percent of the family's peak at step 50 to the full peak at 500 to 600, then falls to 50 percent at 950, because high chroma near white or black is either out of gamut or garish. Hue drifts a few degrees across the scale (yellows warm toward brown in the dark steps, blues toward indigo), which is how natural pigments behave. Any colour outside sRGB is pulled in by reducing chroma at fixed L and hue.

Neutrals have 13 steps (paper `0`, the 11 core steps, plus `850`) so dark mode gets three real surfaces. Light mode reads text at 900, secondary at 700, placeholder at 600, control borders at 500; dark mode mirrors it (text 50, secondary 400, placeholder 500, control border 600).

Steps that matter most: `700` is the action fill and status text in light mode (white text 4.9:1 or better on every family); `800` is wash text; `400` is the vivid fill for marigold-like inks with ink text on top; `100` and `950` are the light and dark washes.

## 7. Data visualisation

Rules (apply to both options):

- **Categorical** colours are fixed GymOS primitives, assigned in order and never cycled. Six slots; a seventh series folds into "Other" (`fg.faint`) or splits into small multiples. Colour follows the entity, not its rank: filtering a series out never repaints the others.
- **Sequential** is one hue light to dark; in dark mode the anchor flips so high values are bright. **Diverging** is cobalt and marigold (the complementary axis, which survives protan and deutan because it is the blue-yellow axis) around a neutral midpoint, for "under target / over target" (calories, weekly change against plan).
- **Status colours are never data colours.** If a series means good or bad (adherence on track or off), it wears status tokens and an icon; otherwise it wears categorical. Never both in one chart.
- Text in charts wears text tokens (`viz.axisLabel` = `fg.secondary`), never the series colour. Direct labels on up to four series, a legend always for two or more, and a data-table view for screen readers.
- **Weight chart**: raw weigh-ins as dots in `viz.categorical.1` at 45 percent opacity, the trend line in `viz.categorical.1` at 2px, the goal band in `viz.goalBand` (moss wash) with a dashed `viz.goalLine`, grid `viz.grid` at 1px, axis labels in tabular figures.
- **Macros** use the first four slots: protein = cobalt, carbs = marigold (wheat, roti), fat = plum, fibre = moss (greens). Those four pass the all-pairs CVD test in both modes.

  scope: categorical palettes only. For a lone status/text color check text contrast with this script's apca() or contrast(); for a sequential ramp, lightness monotonicity.

## 9. Glass option

### 9.1 Where it belongs and where it must not go

| Glass yes                                    | Glass no                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Web top bar (scrolls over content)           | Tables and the roster list                                                             |
| Bottom tab bar (web phone, iOS)              | Forms: intake, vitals, check-in, dietary                                               |
| Client header when it is sticky over the hub | Stat tiles, chart cards, any data-heavy card                                           |
| Sheets and overlays on iOS (system sheets)   | The plan editor                                                                        |
| Command palette scrim on web desktop         | The severe-allergy chip and any status chip (chips sit on an opaque pill inside glass) |

Rule of thumb: glass is for chrome that content scrolls under. It tells the coach "this floats above your work". Anything the coach reads closely, compares or types into stays solid.

### 9.2 Tokens

| Token                                     | Light                                  | Dark                                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| `glass.chrome` (tint)                     | `neutral.0` at the chrome alpha below  | `neutral.900` at the chrome alpha below |
| `glass.sheet` (tint)                      | `neutral.0` at the sheet alpha below   | `neutral.900` at the sheet alpha below  |
| `glass.blur.chrome` / `glass.blur.sheet`  | 20px / 32px                            | 20px / 32px                             |
| `glass.saturate`                          | 1.6                                    | 1.3                                     |
| `glass.border` (inner hairline highlight) | `rgba(255,255,255,0.55)`               | `rgba(255,255,255,0.10)`                |
| `glass.edge` (outer hairline)             | `rgba(22,26,32,0.10)`                  | `rgba(0,0,0,0.45)`                      |
| `glass.highlight` (inner shadow)          | `inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,0.70)` | `inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,0.08)`  |

| `glass.fallback`                          | `bg.surface`                           | `bg.raised`                             |

Noise is a tiled SVG turbulence texture at that opacity; it hides banding in the blur and gives the glass a printed grain that matches the illustration.

### 9.3 Legibility: the numbers

Two tiers, solved by the build script for the lowest tint opacity that passes over the worst backdrop:

- **Chrome** carries only `fg.primary` text and icons (unselected tab labels use `fg.primary` at weight 500; the selected tab label sits on an opaque `action.wash` pill). Held to 7:1 worst-case for headroom.
- **Sheet** carries secondary text, links and inputs. Held to 4.5:1 for every text token, worst-case.

| Option     | Mode  | Tint    | Chrome alpha (fg.primary >= 7:1) | Worst-case text on chrome                 | Sheet alpha (all text >= 4.5:1) | Worst-case text on sheet                   |
| ---------- | ----- | ------- | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| print      | light | #FFFDFA | 0.66                             | primary 7.11, secondary 3.11, action 2.29 | 0.92                            | primary 14.11, secondary 6.18, action 4.54 |
| print      | dark  | #181C22 | 0.76                             | primary 7.22, secondary 3.52, action 3.77 | 0.84                            | primary 9.58, secondary 4.67, action 5.01  |
| floodlight | light | #FCFDFF | 0.66                             | primary 7.12, secondary 3.07, action 2.36 | 0.91                            | primary 13.74, secondary 5.92, action 4.55 |
| floodlight | dark  | #171B28 | 0.76                             | primary 7.19, secondary 3.49, action 3.72 | 0.83                            | primary 9.26, secondary 4.5, action 4.79   |

Backdrops tested per row: 46 (pure black, pure white, and steps 300/500/700/900 of every hue family in the option). A blurred backdrop is an average of what is behind it, so it can never be more extreme than the most extreme solid patch; the solid patches bound the blurred case.

What this means: honest glass is mostly frosted. The chrome tier at 66 to 76 percent tint still shows colour and movement through it; the sheet tier at 83 to 92 percent is close to solid, which is why I recommend solid sheets on web and Android and system sheets on iOS. Secondary text is not allowed on chrome glass at all: at the chrome alpha it drops to about 3:1 over a black patch.

### 9.4 Platform notes

- **iOS.** `expo-blur` wraps `UIVisualEffectView` and offers the system materials (`systemChromeMaterial`, `systemMaterial` and their light and dark variants); every tint adds a translucent layer on top of the blur ([Expo BlurView docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/blur-view/)). On iOS 26, `expo-glass-effect` exposes Liquid Glass through `GlassView`, which falls back to a plain view on older iOS; Expo says to check `isLiquidGlassAvailable()` first ([Expo GlassEffect docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/glass-effect/)). If the native-tabs decision in the report goes ahead, the tab bar gets the system material for free, which is the best glass on any platform here. Use system materials on iOS rather than these custom tints; the tokens matter for web.
- **Android.** `expo-blur` is stable on Android from SDK 55 but needs the content wrapped in a `BlurTargetView`. Efficient blur relies on the RenderNode API from Android 12 (API 31); older versions fall back to RenderScript, which Expo calls "much less efficient", and `blurMethod: 'dimezisBlurViewSdk31Plus'` blurs only on API 31+ and shows a plain translucent view below that ([Expo BlurView docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/blur-view/)). A plain translucent view without blur fails legibility, so below API 31 the fallback must be the solid `glass.fallback`, not the tint. I have no measured share of Pakistani Android devices below API 31 or of low-end GPUs; check app analytics before turning it on. Recommendation: off on Android in the first release.
- **Web.** `backdrop-filter` has 97.25 percent global support per [caniuse](https://caniuse.com/css-backdrop-filter) (Chrome 76+, Safari 9+, Firefox 103+, Samsung Internet 12+); ship `-webkit-backdrop-filter` alongside it. Each blurred layer re-renders what is behind it on every scroll frame, so: at most two glass layers per viewport, blur at or under 20px on phones, never on a long scrolling list itself, and an `@supports not (backdrop-filter: blur(1px))` rule that switches to the solid fallback.

### 9.5 Accessibility

- **Reduce Transparency**: on iOS, `AccessibilityInfo.isReduceTransparencyEnabled()` and the `reduceTransparencyChanged` event are iOS-only ([React Native AccessibilityInfo](https://reactnative.dev/docs/accessibilityinfo)); when on, render `glass.fallback`. On web, `@media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce)` is not Baseline yet ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-transparency)); use it where supported and keep a user setting "Solid bars" in Account for everyone else.
- **High contrast**: `prefers-contrast: more` on web, `isDarkerSystemColorsEnabled()` on iOS and `isHighTextContrastEnabled()` on Android all switch to the solid fallback with `border.control` edges.
- **Focus**: focus rings on glass use `focus.ring` plus a 2px `bg.surface` halo so they stay visible over any backdrop.
- **Motion**: no parallax or animated blur radius; glass appears and disappears with the element, not on its own.

### 9.6 Honest trade-offs and verdict

For: it gives the shell depth and a sense of the content underneath, it is the native idiom on iOS 26, and on web it costs one CSS rule with a fallback. Against: legible glass is mostly opaque, so the effect is subtle; it costs GPU on every scroll frame; it adds a fallback path to test on three platforms; and it fights the flat-print character of Option 1 if overused. **Verdict: offer it as a chrome-only variant (top bar, tab bar, sticky client header), on by default on iOS through system materials, on for web with the solid fallback, off on Android for now. Do not make it the look of the product.**

### 10.2 Urdu: Nastaliq or Naskh

Nastaliq is the style Urdu readers grow up with; Naskh is the more compact style used for Arabic and Persian. iOS has rendered Urdu in Nastaliq since iOS 11, after a long campaign ([Express Tribune](https://tribune.com.pk/story/1514433/apple-finally-enables-nastaleeq-typeface-urdu-keyboard-ios-11)), so a Naskh-only app can feel foreign. Stock Android's Urdu support is less clear: Noto Nastaliq Urdu is in the Android source tree, but users report it missing as a default on some phones ([Samsung community](https://eu.community.samsung.com/t5/other-smartphones/implementation-of-nastaliq-urdu-font/td-p/9651622)), so bundle the font rather than rely on the system.

Vertical metrics I measured from the Google Fonts files (hhea ascent plus descent, in em): Inter 1.21, Archivo 1.09, Noto Naskh Arabic 1.70, **Noto Nastaliq Urdu 2.50**, Gulzar 2.70. Nastaliq needs roughly twice the line height of Latin; Android is reported to clip Nastaliq's first line when line height is too tight ([ProAndroidDev](https://proandroiddev.com/taming-noto-nastaliq-fixing-urdu-font-rendering-issues-in-android-c65e83b40b34)).

Recommended split: **Noto Nastaliq Urdu** for headings, reading text, notifications and explanations (where Urdu should feel like Urdu), and **Noto Naskh Arabic** for dense UI: table cells, chips, inputs, tab labels, buttons, anything inside a fixed-height control. Both OFL, both variable 400 to 700. Gulzar (OFL, 2022, Nastaliq, has `tnum`) is a refined alternative for headings but ships one weight only. Alternatives: Naskh everywhere (dense, consistent, can read as Arabic to Urdu readers) or Nastaliq everywhere (authentic, but doubles row heights and clips in fixed controls).

## 11. Spacing, radius, elevation, border, motion

- **Spacing** (4px base): 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64. The current 10 and 14 steps map to 8/12 and 12/16; keep them as legacy aliases for one release.
- **Radius**: xs 4, sm 6 (chips), md 10 (buttons, inputs, segmented controls), lg 14 (cards), xl 20 (sheets, dialogs), full (avatars, pills). Tenant radius presets: crisp (md 6, lg 10), soft (the default above), round (md 14, lg 20).
- **Elevation**: light cards use a 1px `border.subtle` plus `0 1px 2px rgba(22,26,32,0.06)`; overlays `0 12px 32px -8px rgba(22,26,32,0.22)`. Shadows use the blue-black ink, not pure black. Dark mode uses surface steps and borders, not shadows. iOS uses hairline separators and grouped backgrounds, no card shadows (the critique's heavy native halos).
- **Border**: 1px hairline (native separators `StyleSheet.hairlineWidth`), 1px control border at 3:1, 2px focus ring with a 2px offset, 2px selected outline.
- **Motion** (detailed by the motion work; durations and easings only): instant 80ms, fast 140ms, base 220ms, slow 320ms. Easing standard `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`, decelerate `cubic-bezier(0.05, 0.7, 0.1, 1)` for entering, accelerate `cubic-bezier(0.3, 0, 1, 1)` for leaving. Springs: sheet damping 28 / stiffness 300, press damping 22 / stiffness 420. Reduced motion collapses everything to an 80ms opacity fade.
